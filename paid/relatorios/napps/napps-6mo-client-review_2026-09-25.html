Napps Cooling, Heating & Plumbing

6 Month Marketing Review
March 24 to September 23, 2026, compared with the same stretch of 2025
Prepared by TopServ Digital

Summary

Over the last six months Napps put $51,948 into Google, Local Services Ads (LSA) and Meta, up 32% from $39,476 in the same stretch last year. Most of the extra went into Meta, a channel that was close to zero last year and now brings in steady lead volume, and into Google search. This review covers what that spend produced in calls and leads. We don't have Napps' revenue or booked job numbers, so the return on that spend can't be measured yet. Connecting the two is the first thing we'd like to do next.

Call figures cover Google search call ads (not Performance Max or website phone taps). LSA cost per lead is LSA spend divided by all leads received. Meta 2026 includes 31 technician applicants.

  • Calls are being picked up and running longer. Only 2 of the 90 calls from Google search ads went unanswered, against 41 of 87 last year, and calls over a minute more than doubled.
  • LSA is the lowest cost lead source. Each lead averaged $38, down from $42. Volume was lower in the spring, then came back in line with last year over the summer (228 leads from June to August against 241).
  • Meta added a new source of leads. 150 leads came through the lead campaign at about $78 each (from $129 in May to $58 in June, and between $69 and $87 since), and a separate hiring campaign brought in 31 applicants for HVAC and refrigeration technician roles while the team was recruiting.
  • The website's organic traffic held steady at about 200 visits a month, so the base of people finding Napps on their own did not shrink.

What we've done since July

  • Rebuilt conversion tracking on the website and in Google Ads on September 15, so leads are counted the same way from here on.
  • Tightened the AC search campaign on August 31 with added negative keywords, split ad groups and an updated landing page.
  • Set the search budget to split evenly between HVAC and plumbing on September 15.

Next 90 days

  • Tie leads to booked jobs. Once Napps shares booked job data, we'll report cost per booked job by channel each month, Meta included, and put budget behind what turns into work.
  • Lean on what costs least per lead. LSA leads average about $38, so LSA stays the main plumbing channel, while Google search stays focused on people searching for Napps by name and the highest intent AC and plumbing searches.
  • Tighten the branded search campaign with cost controls, so more budget goes toward new customers.
  • Get a clean read on plumbing search by running it steadily for 30 days and comparing it with LSA.
  • Grow the technician pipeline. The hiring campaign brought in 31 applicants at about $22 each, and with your approval we'd bring it back, since capacity sets how many jobs Napps can take.
  • Plan the spring HVAC push around capacity, lining up Google, LSA and Meta with the technicians Napps expects to have.

What we need from Napps

  • Booked jobs and revenue by month since January 2025, split into HVAC and plumbing, with the lead source for each job if it's tracked. That lets us show which channel pays back and put budget behind it.
  • A short walkthrough of the LSA app together, to go over business hours, job types and how leads get marked, so we send leads your team can take, especially in the summer HVAC rush.
  • Your capacity plan for next season, so we can size the spring HVAC push to it.
