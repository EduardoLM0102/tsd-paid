Weekly Campaign Update

Week of July 25 to July 31, 2026

Channels: Google Ads and Meta Ads

Prepared by TopServ Digital

This week we invested $2,015.31 across Google and Meta and recorded 8 customer leads, plus 9 job applications from the new hiring campaign. Two structural changes went in. Google now bids toward a higher standard of phone call, and the Meta lead campaign came off while new creative is built around the consolidated offer you asked for.

1. What Changed

Google Ads

  • Raised the bar on what counts as a lead. Google was bidding toward any call that lasted 15 seconds. It now optimizes toward calls that run a full minute or more, plus form submissions. Budget follows the people who actually get into a conversation with your office rather than the ones who hang up after a few seconds.
  • Performance Max daily budget brought back to $50. It had been running at $155 a day and the extra spend was buying reach rather than calls.
  • Held the non-branded Cooling campaign off while we get the destination and the tracking right. Branded search stays live and is covering 93% of the people searching for you by name.
  • Requested a dedicated landing page for your search traffic. This is the piece we think moves Google the most, and we are waiting on your approval before we build it. Once it is proven on search we will look at testing it behind the Meta ads as well.

Meta Ads

  • Paused the lead generation campaign on July 30. Cost per lead had climbed to $174.81 for the week against $110.91 across its full run, so we took it down rather than keep paying the higher rate while the next creative batch is produced.
  • Brought the awareness campaign back on at $20 a day. It keeps your name in front of the tri state at $0.68 per person who remembers seeing you, which is the cheapest work in the account.
  • Launched a hiring campaign on July 27 for the Journeyman and Lead Installer roles, targeted 20 miles around Newport. It has brought in 9 applications so far.
  • New creative requested and in production around the consolidated offer you asked for. Expected to go live early next week.

3. Expected Impact

  • The change to how Google counts a lead. Should bring cost per lead down by spending on people who hold a real conversation with your office instead of people who hang up in a few seconds. We will be watching cost per click, cost per call, and call volume.
  • Performance Max budget correction. Frees roughly $105 a day that was buying reach, to be redeployed once the landing page is live. We will be watching cost per click and the share of searches you show up for.
  • Dedicated landing page. The biggest lever available on Google right now. Google charges less per click when the page a searcher lands on matches what they typed, and today your search traffic goes to the general site. Two thirds of the searches we are missing are missed on page and ad quality, not on budget, so a matched page buys more clicks for the same money and lowers cost per lead. It also gives us a form we can measure properly. We will be watching cost per click, ad position, and cost per lead. If it performs on search, the same page becomes a test worth running behind the Meta ads.
  • Consolidated offer creative on Meta. Should bring cost per lead back toward the $47 to $85 range the strongest offers have already hit, instead of the $174.81 the tired creative was running at. We will be watching cost per lead, click rate, and lead quality.
  • Hiring campaign. At $16.23 an application it is the most efficient thing running. We will be watching application volume and whether the candidates are the right level.
