Overview

Westchester is a heating market first. In Google's own search data for the county, heating searches (boiler, furnace and heat pump repair) jump in September, peak in October and stay high straight through February. Cooling only takes over from May to August. This plan launches right into the heating window.

Lead generation runs on Google. Local Services Ads and Search catch people who already have a problem and are looking for someone to fix it, which in a Westchester winter usually means no heat. Meta does a different job. It runs for awareness only, so that when a homeowner's boiler quits, Carey & Walsh is already a name they know.

Carey & Walsh starts with things most of the competition can't say: 80 years in the county, a real specialty in steam and hot-water boilers in a county full of older homes, Mitsubishi Electric Diamond Contractor status, and its own sheet metal shop. The plan puts that in front of people at the moment they're choosing who to call.

Budget note: This plan is built on channel ratios rather than fixed dollars, so it holds at any spend level. Scenarios below are modeled at $4,000, $6,000 and $9,000 per month.

What Each Channel Does

  • Google Local Services Ads (HVAC): Pay per lead, not per click, at the very top of Google with the Google Guaranteed badge, the star rating and a call button. Best channel for repair and emergency demand: no heat, boiler down, AC out. Google has to verify the business first (license, insurance, background checks), which usually takes two to five weeks, so LSA ramps up after Search.
  • Google Ads (Search / PPC): Pay per click on what LSA doesn't cover well, and on the bigger jobs: boiler and furnace replacement, heat pumps, Mitsubishi ductless, oil-to-gas conversions, plus searches for Carey & Walsh by name. Each ad group sends people to the matching service page on careyandwalsh.com.
  • Meta (Facebook & Instagram), awareness only: No lead forms and no discount offers. Video and image ads run on Meta's Ad Recall objective, which shows them to the people in the county most likely to remember them. Most HVAC companies advertising on Meta around Westchester right now are pushing discounts. Carey & Walsh gets to tell a different story: the same local company that's been fixing Westchester's boilers since 1944.

LSA and Google Ads are separate Google products, each with its own budget and reporting.

Who We're Targeting

  • The No-Heat Call: Boiler or furnace quit, often at night or over a weekend, often in an older house. Wants a real person on the phone and a technician tonight. This is who the 24/7 emergency line is for.
  • The Old-House Owner: Steam radiators, a cast-iron boiler, maybe an oil burner in the basement. Has dealt with contractors who don't understand older systems and wants someone who'll say honestly whether it's worth repairing.
  • The Replacement Buyer: The system is at the end of its life. Wants a free estimate, the price in writing, and to hear about financing and rebates before signing anything.
  • The Heat Pump Shopper: Reading about heat pumps and New York incentives, maybe for an addition, maybe to get off oil. Diamond Contractor status and the option to pair a heat pump with the existing boiler speak directly to this person.
  • The Building Manager: Multi-family buildings, offices and retail, where one boiler can serve a whole building and downtime isn't an option. Lower volume, bigger contracts, recurring maintenance.

Logic: Search leads in November because it can go live right away while LSA waits on verification. As LSA comes online and the cold sets in, its share grows, since paying per lead is the cheapest way to buy a no-heat call. By January the two run even, with Search moving part of its budget toward replacement and heat pump estimates, where the bigger tickets are. Meta is heaviest at launch, when nobody in the county has seen a Carey & Walsh ad yet, then tapers. LSA only charges for leads that come in, so any share it can't spend moves to Search that month.

Search split: roughly 65% heating (boiler, furnace, no heat, tune-ups), 25% heat pumps and ductless, 10% brand. Air conditioning campaigns stay off through the winter and come back in the spring plan.

Meta split: 100% awareness on Ad Recall Lift, one always-on campaign. The creative is built for recall, not recycled sales ads: the Briarcliff Manor shop, the technicians, real customer reviews and the 1944 story.

Monthly Messaging

November — First Cold Night

  • Angle: The boiler's been off since April. Find out how it's doing before the first cold night does.
  • Message: Heating season starts this month. A tune-up now catches the problem before it turns into an emergency in January, and a maintenance agreement puts you first in line once it does get cold. If the heat's already out, the emergency line picks up 24/7.
  • Channel focus: Search on heating repair, boiler service and tune-up terms, plus brand; LSA as soon as verification clears; Meta launches the 1944 story countywide.

December — Heating Peak

  • Angle: No heat at 2 a.m., and a real person answers.
  • Message: In a Westchester December, a no-heat call doesn't wait until Monday. Carey & Walsh dispatches around the clock, 365 days a year, holidays included, and most repairs get done the same day.
  • Channel focus: LSA and Search on emergency and repair terms around the clock; Meta on real customer reviews, like the boiler that quit on the coldest night of the year and ran again before midnight.

January — Deep Cold & Replacement

  • Angle: Repair it or replace it. We'll tell you straight.
  • Message: A 40-year-old cast-iron boiler often has years left in it, and we'll say so. When it doesn't, the estimate is free and the price is in writing before anything starts. We'll check which New York incentives and rebates you qualify for, and financing is available. If you're thinking about a heat pump, we can pair one with the boiler you already have instead of pushing a full conversion.
  • Channel focus: LSA stays on emergencies; Search shifts part of its share to replacement, heat pump and ductless terms; Meta on Diamond Contractor status and the shop that builds ductwork to fit older houses.

KPIs

  • Google LSA cost per lead: $50 – $90. HVAC Local Services Ads averaged about $51 a lead nationally this year and $45 to $80 in large metros, with New York near the top. Expect the high end on winter emergency calls.
  • Google Search: CPC $18 – $40, CPL $125 – $275. From Google Keyword Planner for Westchester County: top-of-page bids on searches like "hvac near me" and "hvac repair near me" run $21 to $100. Boiler and furnace repair searches show no bid estimates at all, which usually means few advertisers compete for them. That's Carey & Walsh's strongest work and likely the cheapest leads in the plan. Our own auction data replaces these estimates after 30 days.
  • Meta (awareness only): Not measured on leads. Measured on estimated ad recall lift, reach and frequency, aiming for about three views per person per week, at a planning CPM of $12 – $20. The indirect read is branded search: more people looking up Carey & Walsh by name as the campaign runs.
  • Combined leads: 35 – 65 a month at $6,000, scaling with budget. November runs lighter while LSA clears verification.
  • Response time: answer LSA calls live and messages within 15 minutes, since Google ranks LSA partly on it.
  • Watch metrics: booked-job rate, cost per booked job, and how many leads turn into replacements, heat pumps or commercial work. A $70 lead that becomes a boiler replacement beats one that becomes a tune-up.
