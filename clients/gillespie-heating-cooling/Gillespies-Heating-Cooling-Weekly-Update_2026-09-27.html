Google Ads Weekly Update: September 21 to September 27, 2026

Channel: Google Search Ads. Prepared by TopServ Digital.

This week we made the move to automated bidding we flagged in the last update, and cleaned up a few things alongside it so the account is running as tight as possible going into it. Here's what changed and why.

1. What Changed and Why

Search terms refined (September 22)

What we did: added a handful of search-term filters, terms like "cheap," "quote," "cost," and "how much," so ads stop showing for price-comparison searches. Also removed two keywords that weren't a strong match for the ad group they were sitting in.

Why: someone typing "how much does HVAC cost" is usually still researching, not ready to book. Keeping the budget on searches closer to booking makes every click count for more.

Non-Branded budget focused on your two strongest service lines (September 23)

What we did: paused the AC repair and heat pump ad groups and kept the budget on furnace repair and general HVAC, the two that have brought in the most engagement so far.

Why: a fixed daily budget spread across four ad groups gives each one less room to work. Putting it behind the two with the strongest track record gives Google's system more to learn from where it already performs. AC repair and heat pump are ready to come back as dedicated ad groups once there's budget to run all four at once.

Automated bidding turned on for both campaigns (September 26)

What we did: moved Non-Branded and Branded from manual bidding to Google's Maximize Conversions, its automated bidding system. Every keyword, ad, and landing page carried over exactly as it was. Budgets stayed the same, $65 a day for Non-Branded and $1 a day for Branded.

Why: manual bidding means every adjustment gets made by hand. Automated bidding lets Google's system set bids in real time off the signals it's collecting, which is the direction we called out once the account had settled after the landing page work.

Service-area targeting matched to your real coverage (September 26)

What we did: updated the map your ads target using the ZIP codes, counties, and regions you sent over, across both Kentucky and Ohio.

Why: this lines delivery up with where you actually send trucks, so the budget goes toward homeowners inside your real service area instead of nearby areas outside it.

2. What's Next

  • Let the new bidding system learn. Any campaign switching to automated bidding needs its first week or two to gather data before it settles in, so we'll hold off on further bidding changes until it does.
  • AC repair and heat pump ad groups. Paused for now, ready to bring back once the budget or the results support running all four ad groups at once.
  • Cincinnati coverage. Your service list didn't include Cincinnati proper, so it isn't targeted right now. Let us know if that's part of your coverage area and we'll add the right ZIP codes back in.
  • Branded budget. Still $1 a day with very little search volume behind your business name. We'll review this with your account manager as the account settles into the new bidding system.
