Gillespie's Heating and Cooling

Weekly Campaign Update

Week of August 15 to August 21, 2026

Channels: Google Ads and Meta Ads

Prepared by TopServ Digital

This week's headline was the launch of your Google Search campaigns, running alongside your ongoing Meta lead-generation campaign, which continued delivering steady leads at a lower cost per click than Search.

1. What Changed

Meta Ads

  • The ongoing lead-generation campaign kept running on its single Instant Form ad set covering Northern Kentucky and Cincinnati, homeowners 30+.
  • This week: 11,784 impressions, 241 clicks, and roughly 3 leads at $112.64 per lead, for $337.92 spent.

Google Ads

  • Two Search campaigns went live on August 18: a Branded campaign that protects your business name in search results, and a Non-Branded campaign built around your core services (AC repair, furnace repair, heat pumps, and general HVAC).
  • In the first four days live, the account generated 201 impressions and 12 clicks across Northern Kentucky and the Cincinnati metro, with the first lead already recorded through the new tracking setup.

2. Why It Changed

  • Meta continues to be the steadier lead source while Search ramps up, and its cost per click (about $1.40) is running well below what Search typically costs for a brand-new account still in its early delivery window.
  • The Google launch reflects the campaign build and targeting plan finalized over the past two weeks, covering your full service area from Newport and Florence to Cincinnati. Full lead tracking (form submissions and phone calls) went live alongside the campaigns, so results are measurable from day one instead of catching up later.

3. Expected Impact

  • On Google, impression and click volume typically climbs through the first one to two weeks of delivery as the algorithm learns which searches convert best, so this week's numbers are a starting point rather than a steady state.
  • On Meta, the current single ad set has been reaching the same audience for several weeks now, so a fresh angle would help keep cost per lead from drifting up as reach saturates.

4. Next Steps

  • Add an awareness layer on Meta: the current ad set is running five direct-response creatives (testimonial, seasonal offer, value prop, repair-vs-replace) with no top-of-funnel content building reach ahead of the ask. A short awareness push would build brand recall in the service area and should help hold cost per lead down as the direct-response ads keep working behind it.
  • Sharpen Google ad group targeting: use the real search terms now coming in to concentrate budget on the highest-intent searches.
  • Give the Branded Google campaign more time: brand name search volume is naturally low, so its first impressions may take another week or two to show up.
  • Standard 3-day and 2-week checks: the usual post-launch review points for the new Google campaigns, watching cost per lead as more data comes in.
