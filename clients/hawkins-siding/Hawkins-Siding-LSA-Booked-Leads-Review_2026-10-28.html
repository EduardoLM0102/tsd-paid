Google Local Services: Booked Leads Review

Leads from September 1 to October 27, 2026

Prepared by TopServ Digital

This review answers where the 29 booked leads in the September report came from, since none of them show as booked when looking at the Local Services account. To answer it with records, we pulled every Local Services lead from Google for September 1 to October 27, listened to every recorded call and read every message thread, 96 leads in all. This report shows what we found, lead by lead.

1. Where "Booked" Comes From

  • It's the status on each lead in the Local Services inbox. Whoever answers the lead can mark it Booked, Declined or leave it open. Google's reports simply count those marks.
  • The Hawkins office team answers these leads. On every call and message we reviewed, the person answering was from the Hawkins office (Claudette, Emila and Jack). They took the customer's details, scheduled a free estimate visit and sent the customer an intro card with the consultant's name and number.
  • "Booked" means an estimate visit was scheduled. Local Services doesn't record whether the estimate turned into a signed job, so from here on our reports will call these booked estimates.
  • Where to find them. In the Local Services inbox, set the date range to September and filter the lead status to Booked. Every lead ID in sections 5 and 6 can be matched there.

4. Things Worth Attention

  • Estimates are still being booked, but not marked. No lead has been marked Booked since October 14, yet the office scheduled 5 estimates from Local Services between October 17 and 24. In all, 9 scheduled estimates are still open or declined in the inbox, so Google's booked count is lower than what the office actually booked.
  • 12 real customers with no appointment yet. They're listed in section 6. Two stand out: a McKinney homeowner (September 15) sent her name, address, phone number and a preferred day, and we found no reply in the thread; and an October 26 caller with rotted wood behind her garage had to hang up mid-call and is waiting on a call back.
  • An estimate that may not have happened (October 3, Mineola). The customer asked for a quote on three sides of a shop building, and the office scheduled the visit for the following Tuesday after 1 PM. On October 12 she wrote back: "When will I get an estimate or will you not be able to service this area?" We found no reply after that message.
  • A canceled appointment still marked Booked (October 10). The customer booked a Monday 1 to 2 PM visit for a wall section and a door, then called back the same day to cancel and said he'd call again.
  • A message lead lost over the phone number (October 22). Panels had blown off in the wind and the customer wanted a quote. Local Services messages don't share the customer's number, so the office kept asking for it, and the customer replied "I don't think you guys have your act together" and then "No thank you." Booking the visit right in the message thread avoids this.
  • Calls going to voicemail during business hours. Local Services callers reached the voicemail greeting on a Friday at 3:56 PM (September 4), a Saturday at 9:35 AM (September 5) and a Friday at 2:48 PM (October 23). An existing customer who called on September 25 also said she kept "getting like an automated assistant" when she tried to reach the office.
  • An estimate email that didn't arrive. That same September 25 caller had an estimate sent on September 4 and again on September 8 and couldn't find either one until the office resent it during a 21-minute call. If other customers are missing their estimate emails, those jobs can stall without anyone knowing.
  • 5 leads marked Booked with no appointment. Three message leads never sent their contact details, one caller only wanted a price over the phone for a mobile home, and one caller was a referral who wanted to wait for Damon.
