What we found in the measurement setup

Two separate tracking failures in March 2026

Reviewing the account day by day, the conversion tracking did not degrade gradually. It stopped in two distinct events, ten days apart, and each one took out a complete group of signals at once.

March 10 — the website tags. The schedule button, request estimate link, book now link, call link and contact form tag all recorded normally through March 10, then returned zero on every single day afterward.

March 20 — the Google Business Profile signals. Click-to-call, direction requests and profile visits were still recording healthily on March 19, then stopped completely from March 21 onward. These come from your Business Profile connection rather than your website, which points to that link being broken separately.

Two signals were unaffected and still work today: calls from the ad, and the web lead form. That is useful evidence — your site did not lose tracking wholesale, so this is a recoverable configuration problem rather than a rebuild.

Why this matters for page one. In Phase 1, the signals that later failed accounted for 505 of the 600 recorded leads — 84% of everything the account was capturing. After March the campaigns kept running and spending, but that share of the results was no longer recorded. So the $101.31 Phase 2 figure is a cost per recorded lead, not a true cost per lead. Separately, automated bidding was optimizing toward signals that had gone dark, which does degrade real delivery — CPC rose from $2.78 to $4.00 over the same period. Both effects are genuine and cannot be cleanly separated with the data available.

On the return-on-investment question

We would rather be straightforward than produce a number that looks precise and is not. The Google Ads account has never had revenue attached to it. A ServiceTitan booked-job conversion exists but has never received a single record, and no conversion carries a revenue value. A genuine ROI figure — revenue earned against dollars spent — cannot be calculated from advertising data alone, for any period, going back to the start.
  • What the ad platform can tell us. Spend, reach, clicks, cost per click, and the leads that were recorded — the figures on page one.
  • What it cannot tell us. Which leads became booked jobs, and what those jobs were worth. That lives in your ServiceTitan records, not in Google Ads.
  • How we close the gap. With revenue-by-source from ServiceTitan for this period, we can match it against the spend above and give you a real, defensible ROI answer.

What we recommend

1. Keep PPC paused
No PPC spend since July 17. We do not recommend restarting until measurement is rebuilt and verified — restarting blind would repeat the same problem.
2. Rebuild the tracking
Restore the website conversion tags, connect the ServiceTitan booked-job signal so it actually receives data, and set it as the primary goal the campaigns optimize toward.
3. Then judge the channel
With revenue flowing back into the account, performance becomes provable month over month instead of inferred — and the ROI question answers itself.

Immediate next steps

  • From us. A full tracking audit — account change history for March 2026, your tag manager, and the Business Profile connection — to confirm exactly what changed on March 10 and March 20 and what it takes to restore it.
  • From your team. A revenue-by-source export from ServiceTitan covering December 2025 through July 2026. That is the missing half of the ROI calculation and we cannot produce it from our side.
  • Together. A single reconciled view of spend against booked revenue, and a clear recommendation on whether and how PPC should resume.
