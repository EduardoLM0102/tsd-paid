Don't invent specific-sounding marketing claims — "same-day service," "no overtime surprise," "upfront pricing," generic financing language — as home-services filler when writing client-facing copy or media-plan messaging. Check the client's actual website first.

**Why:** Surfaced on [[tsd-cool-hand-electric]] (2026-08-05). Wrote plausible category-standard messaging into the Aug–Dec media plan without checking coolhandelectric.com. Edu asked afterward whether it was sourced from his intake or the client's site — it was neither, it was generic filler. A live-site check turned up real, better material sitting unused: real financing (Synchrony Bank), three live specials ($100 off surge protection, free whole-home surge protector, $37 electrical safety inspection), a real guarantee line, and a membership program. Genuine specifics would have made the copy sharper *and* accurate; the invented ones just happened to sound plausible.

**How to apply:** before finalizing client-facing copy that references guarantees, service-level promises (same-day, 24/7, response time), specials/offers, or financing, WebFetch the client's site (home page, plus specials/financing pages if they exist) to confirm what's actually true. If nothing specific turns up, keep the messaging generic rather than inventing a specific-sounding claim. Composes with [[tsd-client-facing-voice]] — that memory governs how copy sounds, this one governs whether its specific claims are real.
