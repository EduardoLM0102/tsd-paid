Standing rule for **messages Edu sends to TSD clients or AMs** (ticket replies to Eve etc., and anything he'll paste and send himself). Given while drafting the Neon Monkey impressions/call-drop reply (2026-07-28).

**Keep them SHORT and concise.** Edu repeatedly cut my drafts as "TOO BIG." A ticket reply is a few tight lines: the finding, the numbers that prove it, the fix, what's needed to execute. No preamble, no windup, no recap paragraph.

**No validation framing.** Do NOT write "he's right / wrong," "good catch," "Brock is right," "you're right to push back," or any variant. Edu: *"Stop fucking saying he's right, wrong or whatever."* Just state the diagnosis and the possible solution. Don't editorialize whether the client's read was correct.

**Structure that worked** (the accepted Neon Monkey draft): one line of what happened → the cause (name it plainly, e.g. "budget cut and PMax pause were Andrea's, to match the media plan") → the proof numbers (June→July) → the fix + what you need to run it. That's the whole message.

**Why:** Edu forwards/sends these himself and reads them in a ticket UI. Length and hedging waste his time and the AM's; the validation language reads as filler and softens a message that should just be direct. Pairs with [[tsd-client-facing-voice]] (how it sounds — natural, no AI tells) and [[topserv-client-report-format]] (framing fixes as proactive). This governs **length + no-validation** specifically.

**Also:** don't assert inferences as fact in these messages. On the same ticket I labeled a conversion action "CRM-uploaded calls" and said LSA "wasn't touched" — both were my guesses, not verified. Report pulled numbers as data; flag interpretation as interpretation.

## Prose-level rules (from the Zen Air spend-cut ticket, 2026-07-31)

Edu rewrote my draft; these are the deltas he made. Write this way from the start.

- **No dashes at all.** Not em dashes, not hyphens used as punctuation. He states this explicitly and repeatedly.
- **Drop the leading "I".** "Went through all three accounts", not "I went through all three accounts."
- **No throat-clearing.** Cut "Being straight about it", "And here's the thing", "That said", "Quick context on". Just say the thing.
- **Casual asides in parentheses.** "The leads have been coming from search PPC and LSA (though it's off now)."
- **"though" as a mid-sentence connector**, comma splices allowed, mild double negatives kept ("The money hasn't gone nowhere"). It should read spoken, not written.
- **Fewer paragraph breaks than I default to.** He merges related ideas into one block.

## When the message goes to a CSR who will relay it verbally

- **No tables.** She has to explain it out loud to the client.
- **Strip all jargon.** He cut "destination score", "CRO", "UI/UX" himself. Say the mechanism in plain words instead ("Google charges less per click when the page matches what they searched for").
- **Restate the savings/headline number inside the recommendation paragraph**, not only in the opening line. The recommendation is what the client remembers.
- **Brief the CSR separately** on anything she could be blindsided by (e.g. on Zen Air, that the client himself paused LSA). Keep it out of the message, give it to her as context.

## Honesty calibration — don't over-sanitize

[[topserv-client-report-format]] says frame fixes as proactive and never signal something was wrong. That does **not** mean pretending a campaign was doing something it wasn't. I framed an underperforming lead campaign as purely a brand play; Edu pushed back: *"we can't just ignore that it should be generating leads and its underperforming."*

The working pattern: **name the underperformance plainly → note the spend still bought something real → conclude we don't need it now.** Example that he accepted: campaign is set up to bring in calls and hasn't been pulling its weight, but the money landed on YouTube/display so it did brand exposure work, and we already cover that on Meta, so cut it. Honest, survives a follow-up question, and still doesn't read as a confession.
