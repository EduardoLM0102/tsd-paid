For TSD video scripts, two standing defaults unless Edu says otherwise:

**1. A company videographer travels to the client's location and shoots it.** The client is NOT recording this alone on a phone. Only assume self-recorded/UGC if Edu explicitly says so.

So write the script as a **production document, not just a block of spoken lines**. That means:
- **Shot direction per beat**, not only the fala. Where the camera is, what it sees, what moves.
- **Use the location and the real assets** — branded trucks/vans pulling up, uniforms, tools, the actual attic, the panel, the rooftop, the unit being worked on, real techs and crew.
- **The camera can move:** full-body walk-n-talk, follow shots, reveals, multiple setups in one 45s video (e.g. open outside at the truck → walk in → land at the panel).
- **B-roll under VO is available** — a line can play over footage instead of on-camera. Not every beat needs a face.
- Talking-head still exists but is the floor, not the default. If the script could have been shot on a selfie stick, the videographer is being wasted.

**2. 45 seconds MAXIMUM.** Hard cap, not a target. At natural speech (~130–150 wpm) that's roughly **100–115 words of spoken copy, tops** — budget against that before writing, not after. If it runs long, cut from the middle, never the hook or the CTA.

**Why:** Edu is sending a videographer to the site; scripts written as phone-selfie talking-heads throw away the whole reason for the trip. And TSD's home-services buyer scrolls, so 45s is the ceiling.

**How to apply:** budget words to the 45s cap first, then build the beats around what the camera can actually show on that property. Composes with [[tsd-client-facing-voice]] — production value does NOT license textbook English; the delivery still has to sound like a real person, in the homeowner's vocabulary.

**Not the self-recorded phone format:** owner-self-records-on-a-phone defaults (vertical, up to 60s, "don't memorize, stumble and keep going") do not apply to TSD. Open question never settled for TSD: who speaks — owner, tech, or VO. Ask rather than assume.
